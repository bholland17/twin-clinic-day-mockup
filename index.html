Notes for Reza

What the clinician is asking for, and what each screen needs from the back end.

What the Twin has to be

  1. The best resident. Does the research before the visit.
  2. The best scribe. Listens during the visit and writes the note.
  3. The best APP. Gets the orders done and makes sure there is follow up after the visit.

Before the visit the order on screen is fixed. Patient banner, then the problem list with the bullets that establish each diagnosis and its significant events, then the short story.

The rules behind every screen

  1. The short story is three or four plain sentences, like a colleague telling you about the patient. It sits under the problem list.
  2. The problem list follows the clinician's own format. Numbered problems. Under each, one line per test or event that establishes the diagnosis, written as date, test, finding, oldest first. Stents carry size, type and post dilation. Problems with nothing to add stay as one line. Family history and a line on how the patient lives go at the end. No duplicate codes.
  3. The Twin adds only two things to that list. A status tag on each problem, and a "New" tag on any line added since the last visit.
  4. Detail is one click down, never on the first view.
  5. Every result in the Twin's text is a link. One tap opens the lab value or the line of the report it came from, highlighted. Back returns to the same place.
  6. Say what changed since the last visit. Do not repeat what is the same.
  7. Each finding appears once, on one screen.
  8. Show confidence, and say so when data is thin.
  9. Nothing is ordered or sent without a clinician signing, unless it only speeds up care.
  10. Every item has an owner.
